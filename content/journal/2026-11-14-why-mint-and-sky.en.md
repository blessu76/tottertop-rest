On 13 November we changed our minds about how Tottertop should look. This post is about why, and what it actually took to notice.

## Where we started

The first playable look, from 11 November, was a night sky: a deep navy backdrop, stone-textured blocks, gold accents, a three-step judgement for each landing, a little recoil when a block is placed, and pieces that break off when the tower falls. It looked serious, and it worked as a first pass.

## The polish pass that changed nothing

On 13 November we gave all six screens — home, play, result, settings, language, and release notes — a shared set of card surfaces, softer shadows, and a clearer order of buttons, with one highlighted button per screen. The code was tidy and the tests passed.

Then we put the new screens next to the old ones, and it was hard to say which was which. Same navy, same gold. The polish was real, but nobody looking at it would have seen a difference, and that is the only kind of difference that counts for a game people open for a minute at a time.

## What we did instead

We stopped refining and drew a new direction: bright, cheerful and cute. We mocked up the six screens in two palettes and compared them side by side:

- **Mint and sky** — the background runs from sky blue into cream, with a sunflower-yellow accent.
- **Peach and pink** — the background runs from pink into cream, with a strawberry-coral accent.

Both shared the same ideas: very round corners, chunky buttons with a soft 3D lower edge, candy-coloured blocks (pink, yellow, mint, sky blue, lilac and orange) with no faces on them, and rounded type, using Jua for titles and Gowun Dodum for body text. We chose mint and sky.

## What is next

We are applying the mint-and-sky look to every screen now, which also means bundling those two fonts and making sure the other languages still fit at narrow widths. Nothing here is released yet and there is no date. When the new look is in a build, we will show it here.
