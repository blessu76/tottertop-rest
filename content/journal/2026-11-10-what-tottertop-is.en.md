Tottertop is a physics game you play with one finger, in the small gaps of a day. This is the first post of the journal, so let us start from the basics: what the game is, and why we are making it at all.

## What the game is

A crane swings above a tower. The tower is already wobbling. When you tap once, the crane drops the block it is carrying, and where that block lands decides whether the tower leans a little more or falls over.

That is all of the controls. There are no menus to learn and no items to collect: you watch the crane, work out where the block will land, and pick the moment to tap. When the tower tips, the round ends, and you start a new one.

A round lasts about a minute, and the game is single player. It is planned for iOS and Android, and the site — and the game when it arrives — will be available in 11 languages.

## Why this game

**It is for a break, not an evening.** We do not want a game that swallows an hour. Waiting for a bus, a gap between two tasks, a moment when your brain is full of something else: the game should fit that kind of pause, start instantly, and be able to end cleanly.

**The wobble is the fun.** In most games the physics happen inside the art. Here the physics are the toy: you can see the tower lean, you can guess why it leans, and a failed stack has a cause you can read afterwards. It is the same feeling as watching something real almost balance, and it is what we find most worth playing.

**We are starting small on purpose.** One mechanic — time a tap, watch the tower — is enough to keep us busy, and it keeps the promise to the player simple: nothing to read, nothing to manage. If that one mechanic does not work, no amount of added features will save the game.

## What is not decided yet

The game is in preparation. There is no store page, no release date and no version number, and we will not promise a date. The plan is for the game to be free, supported by rewarded ads you choose to watch, with an optional purchase that removes the ads — that is a plan, not a promise. Details may change as development goes on; when something concrete happens — a build, a name change, a decision — it will be written here.
